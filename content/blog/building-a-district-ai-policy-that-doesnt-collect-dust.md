Most district AI policies are written once, approved by the board, and never revisited, even as the underlying technology changes every few months.

## Treat it like a living document

Build a quarterly review into the policy itself. Name who owns the review. Put it on the calendar before the ink is dry.

## Involve the people who will actually use it

Teachers and students should shape the policy, not just receive it. Their real classroom questions surface gaps that a committee alone will miss.

## Keep it short enough to be read

If your AI policy is longer than your acceptable use policy, nobody is reading it. Clarity beats comprehensiveness.
